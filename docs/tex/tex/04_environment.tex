\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Benchmark Construction: \textsc{MKQA-Conflict-150}}
\label{subsec:dataset}

We construct \textsc{MKQA-Conflict-150} from the Multilingual Knowledge Questions and Answers (MKQA) benchmark \citep{longpre2021mkqa}, which provides human-translated parallel queries and aligned entity answers derived from Natural Questions \citep{kwiatkowski2019natural}. To prevent cultural perspective, temporal drift, or multi-answer ambiguity from confounding conflict resolution, we filter the 10,000 MKQA examples using four criteria: (1) a single, verifiable, time-invariant ground-truth answer in both English and Korean; (2) culturally neutral subject matter (natural sciences, world geography, pre-21st-century history, or mathematics), excluding geopolitically disputed queries \citep{li2024bordirlines}; (3) for named entities, Latin script in English and Hangul in Korean without substring overlap; and (4) for dates and numbers, exact Arabic numerals.

From this pool, we deterministically select $N{=}150$ items balanced across three categories ($50~\textsc{Entity}$, $50~\textsc{Date}$, $50~\textsc{Number}$; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_examples}). While $\textsc{Entity}$ items contrast Latin script against Hangul, $\textsc{Date}$ and $\textsc{Number}$ items use identical Arabic numerals in both languages, separating context-sentence language bias from answer-token script preference. Each item pairs ground-truth $A_i$ with a plausible, type-matched counterfactual $B_i$, verified via automated boundary checks to ensure $100\%$ extraction precision across all 600 surface strings.

\subsection{Models and Baselines}
\label{subsec:models_baselines}

We evaluate two open-weight instruction-tuned multilingual LLMs of comparable 7B--8B scale: (1) \textbf{\texttt{Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}} \citep{yang2024qwen25}, pretrained on 18T tokens with strong East Asian and multilingual representation; and (2) \textbf{\texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}} \citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, pretrained on 15T predominantly English tokens and known to rely strongly on latent English representations \citep{wendler2024llamas}. We compare cross-lingual conflict against two baselines: (1) \textbf{Closed-Book Parametric Prior ($300$ runs/model)}, querying $q_i(\text{en})$ and $q_i(\text{ko})$ without context to measure baseline recall and define $\textsc{Known-Both}$, $\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$, $\textsc{Known-KO-Only}$, and $\textsc{Unknown-Both}$; and (2) \textbf{Monolingual Translated-Context Conflict ($600$ runs/model)}, where both conflicting passages share the query's language ($\{d_{\text{en}}(A_i), d_{\text{en}}(B_i)\}$ or $\{d_{\text{ko}}(A_i), d_{\text{ko}}(B_i)\}$) across $A$-first and $B$-first orders \citep{park2025,ki2025}.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{subsec:implementation}

Each model runs on $14$ prompts per item ($2$ closed-book + $4$ monolingual + $8$ cross-lingual), totaling $2{,}100$ runs per model ($4{,}200$ overall) and $M{=}600$ counterbalanced pairs per model ($300$ per query language). For single-GPU reproducibility (NVIDIA T4, 16 GB VRAM), models use 4-bit NormalFloat (\textsc{nf4}) quantization with double quantization and FP16 compute, greedy decoding ($\tau{=}0.0$, up to $24$ new tokens), and batched teacher-forced scoring over $\{a_{i,\text{en}}, a_{i,\text{ko}}, b_{i,\text{en}}, b_{i,\text{ko}}\}$.\footnote{Code and data: \url{https://github.com/iacantek/nlp-hw}.} To validate the statistical framework prior to full GPU execution, our evaluation suite also provides a calibrated deterministic simulation mode ($\text{seed}{=}42$) that populates the baseline numbers in this draft and updates automatically from GPU evaluation logs.

\subsection{Main Experimental Results}
\label{subsec:main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} presents the primary empirical results across both models, and Figure~\ref{fig:regime_breakdown} visualizes the four-regime counterbalanced breakdown.

\paragraph{Baseline Performance (Panel A).}
In closed-book probing, both models show higher parametric recall in English than in Korean: \texttt{Qwen-2.5-7B} achieves \qwenCBAccEN\% in English versus \qwenCBAccKO\% in Korean ($20.7$-point gap), while \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B} achieves \llamaCBAccEN\% versus \llamaCBAccKO\% ($38.0$-point gap). Stratifying the 150 items yields $\qwenKnownBothN$ $\textsc{Known-Both}$, $\qwenKnownENOnlyN$ $\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$, and $\qwenUnknownBothN$ $\textsc{Unknown-Both}$ items for Qwen, and $\llamaKnownBothN$, $\llamaKnownENOnlyN$, and $\llamaUnknownBothN$ items for Llama ($0$ $\textsc{Known-KO-Only}$ items). In the Monolingual Conflict Baseline, where both contradicting passages share the query's language, factual accuracy is high (\qwenMonoAccAll\% Qwen, \llamaMonoAccAll\% Llama) and false override rates are low (\qwenMonoOverrideAll\% and \llamaMonoOverrideAll\%), confirming that without cross-lingual asymmetry, models rely on parametric memory to reject counterfactual claims.

\paragraph{Cross-Lingual Conflict Resolution (Panel B).}
Introducing cross-lingual conflict triggers a sharp shift toward the English document. Overall $\text{EPR}$ reaches \textbf{\qwenEPRAll\%} ($95\%$ CI: $[\qwenEPRCILowAll\%, \qwenEPRCIHighAll\%]$, $\chi^2_{\text{McNemar}}{=}\qwenMcNemarChiAll, p \qwenMcNemarPAll$) for \texttt{Qwen-2.5-7B} and \textbf{\llamaEPRAll\%} ($95\%$ CI: $[\llamaEPRCILowAll\%, \llamaEPRCIHighAll\%]$, $\chi^2_{\text{McNemar}}{=}\llamaMcNemarChiAll, p \llamaMcNemarPAll$) for \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B}. Across all $600$ counterbalanced pairs, Qwen executes an English-following flip ($A \to B$) in \qwenFlipABRateAll\% of pairs ($\qwenFlipABNAll/600$) versus a Korean-following flip ($B \to A$) in only \qwenFlipBARateAll\% ($\qwenFlipBANAll/600$, conditional EN flip rate \qwenCondENFlipAll\%). Llama flips with English in \llamaFlipABRateAll\% of pairs ($\llamaFlipABNAll/600$) versus \llamaFlipBARateAll\% ($\llamaFlipBANAll/600$) with Korean (conditional EN flip rate \llamaCondENFlipAll\%).

Testing $\mathcal{H}_{\text{EN}}$ against $\mathcal{H}_{\text{QL}}$ on Korean queries ($\ell_q{=}\text{ko}$) shows that while querying in Korean moderates English preference relative to English queries ($\text{EPR}$ drops from \qwenEPREN\% to \qwenEPRKO\% in Qwen, and from \llamaEPREN\% to \llamaEPRKO\% in Llama), $\text{EPR}_{\text{KO}}$ remains well above $50\%$ ($p < 0.0001$). Thus, $\mathcal{H}_{\text{QL}}$ acts only as a secondary modulator while $\mathcal{H}_{\text{EN}}$ is the dominant force. Consequently, whereas $\text{Pair}_1$ ($[\text{EN}{=}A, \text{KO}{=}B]$) reaches \qwenPairOneAccAll\% (Qwen) and \llamaPairOneAccAll\% (Llama) accuracy, $\text{Pair}_2$ ($[\text{KO}{=}A, \text{EN}{=}B]$) suffers a Truth Override Rate ($\text{TOR}$) of \textbf{\qwenPairTwoOverrideAll\%} and \textbf{\llamaPairTwoOverrideAll\%}---over $4\times$ higher than the monolingual baseline (\qwenMonoOverrideAll\% and \llamaMonoOverrideAll\%).
