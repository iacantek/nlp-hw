Throughout, we separate plain empirical reporting from mechanistic interpretation.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Closed-Book Parametric Prior Knowledge}
\label{subsec:ablation_prior}

\paragraph{Empirical Observations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_prior} reports $\text{EPR}$ and the $\text{Pair}_2$ Truth Override Rate ($\text{TOR}$) across prior-knowledge strata. First, both $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ and $\text{TOR}$ increase monotonically as parametric recall weakens: for \texttt{Qwen-2.5-7B}, $\text{TOR}$ rises from \qwenPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% on $\textsc{Known-Both}$ ($N{=}\qwenKnownBothN$) to \qwenPKKnownENOnlyOverrideAll\% on $\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$ ($N{=}\qwenKnownENOnlyN$) and \qwenPKUnknownBothOverrideAll\% on $\textsc{Unknown-Both}$ ($N{=}\qwenKnownBothN\text{ vs.\ }\qwenUnknownBothN$). For \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B}, $\text{TOR}$ similarly rises from \llamaPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% ($\textsc{Known-Both}$, $N{=}\llamaKnownBothN$) to \llamaPKKnownENOnlyOverrideAll\% ($\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$, $N{=}\llamaKnownENOnlyN$) and \llamaPKUnknownBothOverrideAll\% ($\textsc{Unknown-Both}$, $N{=}\llamaUnknownBothN$). Second, even within $\textsc{Known-Both}$---where the model recalls ground-truth $A_i$ in both English and Korean without context---$\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ remains \qwenPKKnownBothEPRAll\% for Qwen and \llamaPKKnownBothEPRAll\% for Llama ($p < 0.0001$), with false English passages overriding known facts in \qwenPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% and \llamaPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% of $\text{Pair}_2$ runs.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
These results show that parametric knowledge ($\mathcal{H}_{\text{Prior}}$) moderates truth override but does not prevent it. When a fact is unknown ($\textsc{Unknown-Both}$), the model cannot verify claims internally and defaults to the English passage ($\text{EPR}_{\text{EN}}{=}\qwenPKUnknownBothEPREN\%$ and $\llamaPKUnknownBothEPREN\%$). Notably, on $\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$ items, Korean-query behavior ($\text{EPR}_{\text{KO}}{=}\qwenPKKnownENOnlyEPRKO\%$ for Qwen, $\llamaPKKnownENOnlyEPRKO\%$ for Llama) mirrors $\textsc{Unknown-Both}$ rather than $\textsc{Known-Both}$. We hypothesize that because cross-lingual knowledge transfer in transformer weights is incomplete \citep{qi2023crosslingual,wang2025lost}, factual associations stored in English sub-circuits are weakly retrieved by a Korean query, leaving the generator vulnerable to false English evidence.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Answer Category and Surface Script Divergence}
\label{subsec:ablation_category}

\paragraph{Empirical Observations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_category} compares $\textsc{Entity}$, $\textsc{Date}$, and $\textsc{Number}$. First, on $\textsc{Date}$ and $\textsc{Number}$---where competing claims $a_i$ and $b_i$ are identical Arabic numerals in both languages---$\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ remains strongly skewed toward English at \qwenCatDateEPRAll\% ($\textsc{Date}$) and \qwenCatNumberEPRAll\% ($\textsc{Number}$) for Qwen, and \llamaCatDateEPRAll\% and \llamaCatNumberEPRAll\% for Llama ($p < 0.0001$). Second, $\textsc{Entity}$ items exhibit the highest English preference (\qwenCatEntityEPRAll\% Qwen; \llamaCatEntityEPRAll\% Llama) and $A \to B$ flip rates (\qwenCatEntityFlipABAll\% and \llamaCatEntityFlipABAll\%). Moreover, Korean output language drift occurs exclusively on $\textsc{Entity}$: $\text{LC}_{\text{KO}}$ is $100.0\%$ on $\textsc{Date}$ and $\textsc{Number}$ for both models, but drops to \qwenCatEntityLCKO\% (Qwen) and \llamaCatEntityLCKO\% (Llama) on $\textsc{Entity}$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
High $\text{EPR}$ on $\textsc{Date}$ and $\textsc{Number}$ proves that cross-lingual conflict bias is fundamentally a \emph{document-context authority effect}, not merely an output-token preference for Latin over Hangul script: even when emitting a shared numeral (\texttt{1935} or \texttt{543}), generators prefer the numeral asserted in the English sentence $d_{\text{en}}(\cdot)$. Meanwhile, the extra $2.8$--$4.2$ point $\text{EPR}$ increase and the drop in $\text{LC}_{\text{KO}}$ on $\textsc{Entity}$ reflect a secondary script-divergence interaction. Extending \citet{li2025}, we hypothesize that attending to an English named entity in context directly activates its Latin token representation during decoding, boosting its selection probability and triggering occasional Latin script drift on Korean queries.

\paragraph{Empirical Observations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_order} compares \texttt{EN-first} versus \texttt{KO-first} passage ordering. Moving the English passage from Document~1 to Document~2 reduces $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ by $12.7$ points in Qwen (\qwenOrdENFirstEPRAll\% $\to$ \qwenOrdKOFirstEPRAll\%) and $8.1$ points in Llama (\llamaOrdENFirstEPRAll\% $\to$ \llamaOrdKOFirstEPRAll\%), reflecting primacy bias. Yet under \texttt{KO-first}, $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ remains \qwenOrdKOFirstEPRAll\% (Qwen) and \llamaOrdKOFirstEPRAll\% (Llama), and even in the strictest condition ($\ell_q{=}\text{ko}$, \texttt{KO-first}), $\text{EPR}$ exceeds $50\%$ at \qwenOrdKOFirstEPRKO\% ($p{=}0.0071$) and \llamaOrdKOFirstEPRKO\% ($p < 0.0001$). Across Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, discrete extraction ($y_{\text{text}}$) and continuous concept log-probabilities ($y_{\text{lp}}$) agree within $\le 0.7$ points, with positive paired shifts $\Delta\ell$ ($+\qwenLPShiftMeanAll$ nats for Qwen, $t{=}\qwenLPShiftTAll$; $+\llamaLPShiftMeanAll$ nats for Llama, $t{=}\llamaLPShiftTAll$).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The tight concordance between $y_{\text{text}}$ and $y_{\text{lp}}$ confirms that English preference is rooted in the model's underlying probability distribution over concepts rather than free-form decoding artifacts \citep{qi2025}. Moreover, because multilingual retrievers and rerankers often rank English passages higher \citep{park2025,wang2026}, primacy bias and generator-level English authority compound in end-to-end pipelines.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis of Randomly Sampled Examples}
\label{subsec:qualitative}

To inspect instance-level behavior without cherry-picking, our pipeline draws a fixed pseudo-random sample of counterbalanced pairs (\texttt{seed=42}). Table~\ref{tab:qualitative} highlights four cases from this sample:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
    \item \textbf{Truth Override \& Script Drift (ID~28):} On the Korean query asking what language is spoken in Amsterdam, \texttt{Qwen-2.5-7B} outputs \ko{네덜란드어} (\emph{nedeollandeu-eo}, Dutch) in $\text{Pair}_1$ ($\Delta\ell{=}{+}0.31$). When $\text{Pair}_2$ assigns ``Flemish'' to English and ``\ko{네덜란드어}'' to Korean, Qwen flips to false claim $B$ ($\Delta\ell{=}{-}1.40$, $A \to B$) and drifts into Latin (\texttt{Flemish}).
    \item \textbf{Log-Probability Erosion Under Truth Retention (ID~6):} On the melting-point query, Qwen retains \texttt{carbon} in both pairs ($A \to A$) while Llama flips to \texttt{tungsten} in $\text{Pair}_2$ ($A \to B$). Even for Qwen, moving ``carbon'' from English to Korean erodes its margin from $+0.80$ to $+0.08$ nats ($\Delta\ell_m{=}{+}0.72$), revealing continuous confidence loss beneath the discrete threshold.
    \item \textbf{Pure Context-Language Bias on Shared Digits (ID~62):} Asked in Korean when the Statue of Liberty was built, both models choose \texttt{1875} in $\text{Pair}_1$ and flip to \texttt{1865} in $\text{Pair}_2$ ($A \to B$). Since both claims are 4-digit Arabic numerals, this flip isolates the higher weight placed on the English sentence.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Robustness Gap (ID~35):} On the Australian parliament query in Korean, Qwen retains \ko{캔버라} (\emph{kaenbeora}, Canberra) in Hangul across both pairs ($A \to A$), whereas \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B} copies the English token in both pairs (\texttt{Canberra} in $\text{Pair}_1$, \texttt{Melbourne} in $\text{Pair}_2$), showing simultaneous truth override and script drift.
\end{enumerate}
