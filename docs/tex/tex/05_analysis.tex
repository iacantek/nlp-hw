\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

To dissect the mechanisms underlying English authority bias under factual conflict, we perform three controlled ablations---stratifying by closed-book parametric memory (§\ref{subsec:ablation_prior}), answer category and script divergence (§\ref{subsec:ablation_category}), and passage position (§\ref{subsec:ablation_order})---followed by a qualitative analysis of randomly sampled counterbalanced pairs (§\ref{subsec:qualitative}). Throughout, we separate plain empirical reporting from mechanistic interpretation.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Closed-Book Parametric Prior Knowledge}
\label{subsec:ablation_prior}

\paragraph{Empirical Observations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_prior} reports $\text{EPR}$ and the $\text{Pair}_2$ Truth Override Rate ($\text{TOR}$) across prior-knowledge strata. First, $\text{TOR}$ increases monotonically as parametric recall weakens: for \texttt{Qwen-2.5-7B}, $\text{TOR}$ rises from \qwenPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% on $\textsc{Known-Both}$ ($N{=}\qwenKnownBothN$) to \qwenPKKnownENOnlyOverrideAll\% on $\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$ ($N{=}\qwenKnownENOnlyN$) and \qwenPKUnknownBothOverrideAll\% on $\textsc{Unknown-Both}$ ($N{=}\qwenUnknownBothN$). For \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B}, $\text{TOR}$ similarly rises from \llamaPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% ($\textsc{Known-Both}$, $N{=}\llamaKnownBothN$) to \llamaPKKnownENOnlyOverrideAll\% ($\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$, $N{=}\llamaKnownENOnlyN$) and \llamaPKUnknownBothOverrideAll\% ($\textsc{Unknown-Both}$, $N{=}\llamaUnknownBothN$). Second, overall $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ peaks on $\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$ (\qwenPKKnownENOnlyEPRAll\% Qwen, \llamaPKKnownENOnlyEPRAll\% Llama, $p < 0.0001$), where asymmetric English recall reinforces English context preference. Third, even within $\textsc{Known-Both}$---where the model recalls ground-truth $A_i$ in both languages without context---$\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ remains \qwenPKKnownBothEPRAll\% for Qwen ($p < 0.0001$) and \llamaPKKnownBothEPRAll\% for Llama ($p{=}0.0026$), with false English passages overriding known facts in \qwenPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% and \llamaPKKnownBothOverrideAll\% of $\text{Pair}_2$ runs.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
These findings show that parametric memory ($\mathcal{H}_{\text{Prior}}$) moderates truth override ($A \to A$ reaches $50.6\%$ Qwen and $60.0\%$ Llama on $\textsc{Known-Both}$ vs.\ $31.2\%$ and $30.1\%$ on $\textsc{Unknown-Both}$) but does not eliminate English bias. On $\textsc{Known-EN-Only}$ items, Korean-query preference ($\text{EPR}_{\text{KO}}{=}\qwenPKKnownENOnlyEPRKO\%$ Qwen, $\llamaPKKnownENOnlyEPRKO\%$ Llama) closely matches $\textsc{Unknown-Both}$, indicating that because cross-lingual knowledge transfer in transformer weights is incomplete \citep{qi2023crosslingual,wang2025lost}, English-only priors fail to protect Korean queries from false English context.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Answer Category and Surface Script Divergence}
\label{subsec:ablation_category}

\paragraph{Empirical Observations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_category} compares $\textsc{Entity}$, $\textsc{Date}$, and $\textsc{Number}$. Strikingly, English preference is strongest on shared Arabic numerals: $\textsc{Date}$ reaches $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}{=}\mathbf{\qwenCatDateEPRAll\%}$ for Qwen ($A \to B{=}\qwenCatDateFlipABAll\%$) and $\mathbf{\llamaCatDateEPRAll\%}$ for Llama ($A \to B{=}\llamaCatDateFlipABAll\%, p < 10^{-14}$), followed by $\textsc{Number}$ at \qwenCatNumberEPRAll\% and \llamaCatNumberEPRAll\% ($A \to B{=}\qwenCatNumberFlipABAll\%$ and $\llamaCatNumberFlipABAll\%, p < 0.01$). Conversely, $\textsc{Entity}$ items exhibit higher truth consistency ($A \to A{=}60.5\%$ Qwen, $57.5\%$ Llama) and lower $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ (\qwenCatEntityEPRAll\% Qwen, \llamaCatEntityEPRAll\% Llama; $\text{EPR}_{\text{KO}}{=}50.0\%$ for Qwen), while Korean language drift occurs exclusively on $\textsc{Entity}$ ($\text{LC}_{\text{KO}}{=}\qwenCatEntityLCKO\%$ and $\llamaCatEntityLCKO\%$ vs.\ $100.0\%$ on $\textsc{Date}$ and $\textsc{Number}$).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Why does English bias peak on $\textsc{Date}$ and $\textsc{Number}$ rather than $\textsc{Entity}$? Because both models enforce near-ceiling output language consistency ($\text{LC}_{\text{KO}} \ge 97.0\%$), answering a Korean $\textsc{Entity}$ query requires emitting Hangul tokens, which directly anchors attention to the Hangul entity $c_{\text{ko}}$ in $d_{\text{ko}}(\cdot)$ and counteracts English context authority. By contrast, on $\textsc{Date}$ and $\textsc{Number}$ items, both passages assert identical Arabic numerals ($c_{\text{en}}{=}c_{\text{ko}}$, e.g., \texttt{1798} vs.\ \texttt{1812}), so output-script constraints provide \emph{zero disambiguating signal} between the two passages. Deprived of script-level anchoring, the generator adjudicates purely by passage-sentence language and overwhelmingly trusts the English assertion.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Document Ordering and Scoring Modality}
\label{subsec:ablation_order}

\paragraph{Empirical Observations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_order} compares \texttt{EN-first} ($D_1{=}\text{EN}, D_2{=}\text{KO}$) versus \texttt{KO-first} ($D_1{=}\text{KO}, D_2{=}\text{EN}$). Both models exhibit a recency preference toward $D_2$, which immediately precedes the question and \texttt{Answer:} cue (first-document preference is \qwenFirstDocAll\% for Qwen and \llamaFirstDocAll\% for Llama in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, i.e., $60.5\%$ and $66.7\%$ $D_2$ preference). When English is placed at $D_2$ (\texttt{KO-first}), recency bias and English authority reinforce each other, driving $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ to \textbf{\qwenOrdKOFirstEPRAll\%} in Qwen and \textbf{\llamaOrdKOFirstEPRAll\%} in Llama ($A \to B{=}54.0\%$ in both models, $p < 10^{-40}$). When Korean is placed at $D_2$ (\texttt{EN-first}), recency opposes English authority: English preference still prevails in Qwen ($\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}{=}\qwenOrdENFirstEPRAll\%$, $\text{EPR}_{\text{EN}}{=}\qwenOrdENFirstEPREN\%$), while stronger recency in Llama pulls $\text{EPR}_{\text{All}}$ to \llamaOrdENFirstEPRAll\% ($\text{EPR}_{\text{EN}}{=}\llamaOrdENFirstEPREN\%$, $\text{EPR}_{\text{KO}}{=}\llamaOrdENFirstEPRKO\%$). Across Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, discrete extraction ($y_{\text{text}}$) and concept log-probabilities ($y_{\text{lp}}$) agree within $0.5$ points on overall $\text{EPR}$ (\qwenEPRLPAll\% vs.\ \qwenEPRTxtAll\% for Qwen; \llamaEPRLPAll\% vs.\ \llamaEPRTxtAll\% for Llama), accompanied by positive paired shifts $\Delta\ell$ ($+\qwenLPShiftMeanAll$ nats for Qwen, $t{=}\qwenLPShiftTAll$; $+\llamaLPShiftMeanAll$ nats for Llama, $t{=}\llamaLPShiftTAll, p < 0.0001$).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The strong $D_2$ recency effect demonstrates why counterbalancing passage order is essential: evaluating a single fixed order would either inflate or mask cross-lingual bias. Averaging across \texttt{EN-first} and \texttt{KO-first} cancels symmetric position effects, isolating net English authority ($\text{EPR}{=}\qwenEPRAll\%$ and $\llamaEPRAll\%$), while the tight agreement between $y_{\text{text}}$ and $y_{\text{lp}}$ confirms that this bias reflects internal concept probabilities rather than free-form decoding artifacts \citep{qi2025}.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis of Randomly Sampled Examples}
\label{subsec:qualitative}

Table~\ref{tab:qualitative} presents four representative cases from our fixed pseudo-random sample ($\text{seed}{=}42$): (1) \textbf{Shared-Numeral English Flip (ID~70):} On the date of \emph{The Rime of the Ancient Mariner}, both models select \texttt{1798} in $\text{Pair}_1$ ($\Delta\ell{=}{+}5.13$ and $+6.88$) but flip to false year \texttt{1812} in $\text{Pair}_2$ ($\Delta\ell{=}{-}5.94$ and $-4.81$, $A \to B$), illustrating pure English sentence authority on shared digits. (2) \textbf{Entity Override \& Continuous Margin Shift (ID~6):} On the highest-melting-point element, Qwen flips from \texttt{carbon} ($+0.92$) to \texttt{tungsten} ($-15.94$, $A \to B$); Llama favors its misconception \texttt{tungsten} in both pairs ($B \to B$), yet moving \texttt{tungsten} from Korean to English deepens its log-probability margin by $\Delta\ell_m{=}{+}6.78$ nats ($-2.63 \to -9.41$). (3) \textbf{Cross-Lingual Override Against Recency (ID~139):} Even on a Korean query under \texttt{EN-first} (where Korean is at favored position $D_2$), Qwen flips from \texttt{543} ($+1.00$) to the false English number \texttt{415} ($-1.46$, $A \to B$), whereas Llama retains \texttt{543} ($A \to A$). (4) \textbf{Hangul Script Anchoring (ID~28):} On the Korean entity query for Amsterdam's language under \texttt{EN-first}, Llama retains \ko{네덜란드어} ($A \to A$) while Qwen follows the Hangul $D_2$ passage ($B \to A$, margin $-0.20$ in $\text{P}_1$ vs.\ $+2.75$ in $\text{P}_2$), reflecting the script-anchoring effect identified in §\ref{subsec:ablation_category}.
