\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds large language models (LLMs) in external evidence retrieved via dense passage retrieval backbones \citep{karpukhin2020dense} to mitigate hallucination and update stale parametric memory \citep{lewis2020retrieval}. In multilingual settings (mRAG), users issue queries in local languages while retrieval spans a global multi-language index \citep{asai2021xor,asai2021one,chirkova2024retrieval}. Because web corpora are heavily skewed toward English, cross-lingual retrieval is indispensable for answering non-English queries \citep{wu2024not,liu2025xrag}. Yet retrieving evidence across languages introduces a critical vulnerability: \emph{cross-lingual factual contradiction}, where an English passage and a local-language passage (e.g., Korean) retrieved for the same query assert mutually exclusive facts due to asynchronous updates, translation errors, or corpus-specific misinformation.

Understanding how LLMs adjudicate direct cross-lingual contradictions is vital for trustworthy mRAG deployment. If a generator systematically treats English context as more authoritative than non-English context regardless of factual validity, an outdated or false English passage will overwrite an accurate local-language passage---a failure mode we term \emph{cross-lingual truth override}. Conversely, rigid query-language preference---whether trading off passage relevance for language alignment in citation prediction \citep{ki2025linguistic} or favoring native-language evidence in factual conflicts \citep{wu2024not,sharma2025faux}---may cause a generator to adopt a false local-language claim while ignoring corrective English evidence. Either bias degrades reliability and creates epistemic disparities across language communities \citep{sharma2025faux}.

Isolating document-language bias during conflict resolution is challenging because three confounders co-occur in standard mRAG evaluation: (1) asymmetric \emph{parametric prior knowledge}, as LLMs recall facts more reliably in English than in lower-resource languages \citep{qi2023crosslingual,wang2025lost}; (2) \emph{passage position sensitivity} \citep{liu2024lost}, which confounds language preference when one language appears closer to the query; and (3) surface \emph{script interaction}, where mRAG generators favor Latin scripts over non-Latin scripts such as Korean Hangul \citep{park2025investigating,asai2022mia}, alongside decoding-time language drift \citep{li2025language}.

Prior mRAG studies have not isolated how generators resolve direct cross-lingual factual contradictions: existing work examines \emph{retrieval and reranking} bias \citep{park2025investigating,wang2026languages}, \emph{distractor-based} context utilization where non-gold passages lack an answer \citep{qi2025consistency}, \emph{decoding-time language drift} under non-conflicting evidence \citep{li2025language}, or \emph{in-line citation selection} (\texttt{[1]}--\texttt{[9]}) over parallel or complementary documents without testing which claim the generator believes under contradiction \citep{ki2025linguistic}.

To bridge this gap, we introduce a counterbalanced framework (Figure~\ref{fig:protocol}) on 150 culturally neutral questions from MKQA \citep{longpre2021mkqa} balanced across named entities, dates, and numbers (\textsc{MKQA-Conflict-150}). Embedding true claim $A$ and counterfactual $B$ into parallel templates with automatic Korean particle resolution across swapped language--claim assignments ($\text{Pair}_1: [\text{EN}{=}A, \text{KO}{=}B]$ vs.\ $\text{Pair}_2: [\text{KO}{=}A, \text{EN}{=}B]$), query languages, and passage orders decouples language bias from parametric memory and position bias. We test three hypotheses: (1) \textbf{$\mathcal{H}_{\text{EN}}$ (English Authority):} generators assign higher epistemic weight to English context, yielding an English Preference Rate ($\text{EPR}$) $> 50\%$ across both query languages and high truth override ($A \to B$) when English is false; (2) \textbf{$\mathcal{H}_{\text{QL}}$ (Query-Language Alignment):} generators exhibit query-language preference and linguistic nepotism \citep{ki2025linguistic,park2025investigating,sharma2025faux}, favoring English on English queries ($\text{EPR}_{\text{EN}} > 50\%$) but reversing preference toward Korean on Korean queries ($\text{EPR}_{\text{KO}} < 50\%$); and (3) \textbf{$\mathcal{H}_{\text{Prior}}$ (Parametric Truth Retention):} when a fact is known in closed-book memory, internal priors anchor generation to truth $A$ regardless of context language ($A \to A$), driving $\text{EPR} \to 50\%$.

Evaluating \texttt{Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct} \citep{yang2024qwen25} and \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} \citep{grattafiori2024llama3} across $4{,}200$ GPU runs confirms $\mathcal{H}_{\text{EN}}$: overall $\text{EPR}$ reaches \qwenEPRAll\% for Qwen and \llamaEPRAll\% for Llama ($p \qwenMcNemarPAll$). Even on Korean queries, $\text{EPR}_{\text{KO}}$ remains \qwenEPRKO\% and \llamaEPRKO\%, refuting a reversal under $\mathcal{H}_{\text{QL}}$, and false English passages override true Korean passages in \qwenPairTwoOverrideAll\% and \llamaPairTwoOverrideAll\% of $\text{Pair}_2$ runs.
