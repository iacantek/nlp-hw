# Presentation Principles Checklist

Use this checklist to structure the narrative arc, slide density, and oral pacing of any research proposal or final paper presentation.

## 1. Narrative Arc & Research Loop
- [ ] **Early Core Framing (Slides 2–3)**: State the **Motivation** (why the problem matters + the specific gap in prior work), the **Research Question**, and the **Hypothesis** (along with what empirical outcome would prove the hypothesis wrong) within the first two content slides.
- [ ] **Complete Research Loop Mapping**: Explicitly walk the audience through:
  1. **Prediction**: Concrete expected behavior on the experimental task and explicit falsification outcomes.
  2. **Design**: Experimental protocol, counterbalancing/crossover setup, dataset filtering, models, and confound controls.
  3. **Planned / Executed Analysis**: Exact response measurement, primary evaluation metric, and statistical significance test.
  4. **Expected / Final Claim & Limitations**: What the result proves for the field, plus honest scope boundaries.

## 2. Claim-Driven Slide Design (Assertion–Evidence Principle)
- [ ] **One Clear Topic + One Full Claim Sentence per Slide**: Every content slide pairs a concise 1-line topic title with a full declarative claim-sentence subtitle stating the takeaway of that slide.
- [ ] **Visual Evidence Over Wall-of-Text**: Pair bullet cards with a large, high-resolution figure, table, or `(planned)` experimental architecture diagram on every content slide (except dense Q&A backup tables).
- [ ] **Concise Bullet Cards**: Limit cards to 2–3 structured bullet points per card (`• Bold Label: concise explanation`) so the audience can scan each point in under 5 seconds.

## 3. Classroom Readability
- [ ] **Back-of-the-Room Legibility**: Never drop body text below `14pt` or table text below `12pt`.
- [ ] **High Contrast**: Use dark ink (`#0F172A` / `#334155`) on a pure white (`#FFFFFF`) or very soft tint (`#F8FAFC` / `#EFF6FF`) background.

## 4. Oral Delivery & Q&A Readiness
- [ ] **7-Minute Pacing**: Each slide's speaker notes must be 3–4 natural spoken sentences (`~75–110 words`, `< 1 minute` per slide), summing to roughly `6.5–7.0 minutes` across the main deck.
- [ ] **Anticipate Reviewer Critiques**: Include a dedicated `[Backup]` slide at the end of the deck that lists methodological/scope critiques alongside confirmed author answers and `TBC` markers for open items.
