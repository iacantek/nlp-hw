\section{Research Idea}
\label{sec:research_idea}

\subsection{Research Idea}

This research paper investigates whether multilingual Large Language Models (LLMs) assign disparate epistemic weight to evidence based solely on its language when resolving direct factual contradictions in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

\subsection{Research Question}

When a multilingual LLM is presented with two semantically equivalent but factually conflicting claims in different languages, does it systematically favor the claim presented in English or the query language, independent of its internal parametric knowledge?

\subsection{Motivation and Contribution}

While recent literature establishes that mRAG systems preferentially cite English documents \cite{ki2025, park2025}, it remains unclear whether this stems from an implicit assumption that English text possesses higher epistemic authority. If models implicitly trust English evidence over low-resource language evidence, mRAG deployments in non-English regions face a critical vulnerability: English misinformation could systematically override correct localized facts. This project contributes a novel, counterbalanced evaluation framework that forces zero-sum factual contradiction across languages. By reversing the language of true and false claims, we cleanly isolate "Language-as-Authority" bias from parametric factual knowledge, providing a rigorous diagnostic tool for multilingual model reliability.
