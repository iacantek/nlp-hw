\section{Research Idea}
\label{sec:research_idea}

\subsection{Research Idea}

This research paper investigates whether multilingual Large Language Models (LLMs) give more credibility to evidence based solely on its language when resolving factual contradictions in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

\subsection{Research Question}

When a multilingual LLM is presented with conflicting factual claims in different languages, does it favor the claim written in English or the query language, regardless of its prior knowledge?

\subsection{Motivation and Contribution}

While recent studies show that multilingual RAG (mRAG) systems preferentially cite English documents \cite{ki2025, park2025}, it remains unclear whether models trust English sources more. If models implicitly favor English evidence over evidence in other languages, deploying mRAG in non-English regions carries a real risk: English misinformation could override accurate local facts. This study develops a balanced benchmark pairing contradictory facts across languages to test model preferences. By reversing the language of true and false claims, the framework isolates \enquote{Language-as-Authority} bias from prior knowledge, providing a diagnostic tool for multilingual model reliability.
